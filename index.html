01 / Overview

Unify the motion space.

Grippers and manipulated objects share one prediction interface.

Robot demonstrations differ in their action spaces, camera poses, and manipulation modes. PointPilot represents motion through task-focused objects: the gripper itself during approach, or a locally rigid object part coupled to it during manipulation.

A memory-conditioned vision–language model plans subtasks, grounds the relevant objects, and conditions a 3D point-flow predictor. Observations and predicted motion are aligned in a shared reference-camera frame. A non-learned geometric adapter maps this flow to end-effector targets for closed-loop execution.

02 / Method

From intent to executable motion.

Planning, grounding, and flow prediction connect through a common 3D frame.

01

Plan the next subtask

A memory-conditioned Qwen2.5-VL planner identifies current and next subtasks for each hand.

02

Ground task-focused objects

Camera-aware 3D grounding predicts object boxes and execution roles: left, right, or both grippers.

03

Predict and execute flow

Point Flow DiT predicts relative point displacements and gripper states. Geometry converts motion into end-effector commands.

↻

Observe, execute, replan. Updated observations close the loop after each action chunk.

Scope & limitations

The current framework focuses on locally rigid task-focused objects and gripper-based execution under stage-local coupling. Non-coupled, friction-driven interactions remain challenging. Dexterous hands, changing contacts, and larger-scale human hand–object data are directions for future work.
